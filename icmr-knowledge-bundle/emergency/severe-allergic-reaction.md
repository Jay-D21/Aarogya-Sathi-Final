# Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis) Protocol

## Overview & Anaphylaxis Recognition
Anaphylaxis is an acute, life-threatening multi-system allergic reaction causing vascular collapse and acute respiratory obstruction within minutes of allergen exposure.

## Diagnostic Criteria
| System Affected | Hallmark Clinical Signs |
|---|---|
| Cutaneous & Mucosal | Generalized urticaria (itching wheals), angioedema (swelling of lips, tongue, uvula, periorbital tissue) |
| Respiratory | Stridor, hoarseness, sudden wheeze, persistent dry cough, intercostal retractions |
| Cardiovascular | Hypotension (systolic BP <90 mmHg or >30% baseline drop), syncope, severe tachycardia |
| Gastrointestinal | Severe cramping abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, incontinence |

*Diagnostic confirmation: Acute onset of mucocutaneous symptoms combined with respiratory distress OR hemodynamic shock.*

## Severity Classification
- **Mild**: Localized rash, pruritus, mild lip swelling without breathing difficulty or systemic spread.
- **Moderate**: Widespread hives, mild wheeze or cough responsive to bronchodilators, preserved blood pressure.
- **Severe (Anaphylaxis)**: Upper airway stridor, severe dyspnea, cyanosis, hypotension, or loss of consciousness.

## Common Triggers in India
- **Insect Stings**: Honeybee (*Apis dorsata* / *Apis cerana*), yellow wasp, and ant stings in fields or households.
- **Medications**: Non-prescription NSAIDs, beta-lactam antibiotics, radiographic contrast media, or adulterated herbal formulations.
- **Foods**: Peanuts, chickpeas (chana/besan), mustard seeds, cow milk, tree nuts, and prawns/shellfish along coastal regions.
- Cultural risk: Avoid applying limestone (chuna), cow dung, or irritating herbal poultices to insect stings.

## Immediate Action & Call 112 Protocol
1. **Call 112 Immediately**: Alert EMS to an active anaphylaxis emergency.
2. **First-Line Pharmacotherapy**: Intramuscular (IM) epinephrine into the anterolateral mid-thigh is life-saving; your doctor may consider prescribing an epinephrine auto-injector for individuals at documented risk.
3. **Positioning**: Lay patient flat with legs elevated to sustain venous return; if dyspneic, allow them to recline comfortably.
4. **Remove Trigger**: Scrape away insect stinger gently without squeezing venom sac; cease offending food or drug.
5. **DO NOT Give Oral Medications or Water**: Pharyngeal edema impairs swallowing and triggers acute airway obstruction.

## ⚠️ Emergency Signs — Call 112
- Hoarseness, stridor, or feeling that the throat is clamping shut
- Cyanosis (bluish tint around lips, tongue, or fingers)
- Dizziness, cold extremities, or sudden collapse
- Rapidly progressive facial and tongue swelling
