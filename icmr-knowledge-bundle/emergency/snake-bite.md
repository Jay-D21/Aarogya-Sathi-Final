# Snake Bite Emergency Protocol

## Overview & Common Indian Venomous Snakes
Over 50,000 fatal snakebites occur annually in India. Over 90% of severe bites are caused by the "Big Four" species:
1. **Spectacled Cobra (*Naja naja*)**: Neurotoxic & cytotoxic venom; postsynaptic neuromuscular paralysis.
2. **Common Krait (*Bungarus caeruleus*)**: Potent presynaptic neurotoxin; painless nocturnal bites with early morning respiratory failure.
3. **Russell's Viper (*Daboia russelii*)**: Vasculotoxic & nephrotoxic venom; coagulopathy, severe pain, hematuria, and renal failure.
4. **Saw-scaled Viper (*Echis carinatus*)**: Vasculotoxic venom; severe local swelling, incoagulable blood, and hemorrhagic shock.

## Diagnostic Criteria & Syndromic Classification
| Envenomation Syndrome | Principal Species | Cardinal Signs & Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| Neurotoxic | Common Krait, Spectacled Cobra | Ptosis (eyelid droop), diplopia, dysphagia, intercostal/diaphragmatic paralysis |
| Vasculotoxic / Hemotoxic | Russell's Viper, Saw-scaled Viper | Persistent oozing from bite, 20-min WBCT >20 min, gum bleeding, hematuria |
| Cytotoxic / Local Tissue | Cobra, Russell's Viper | Rapidly progressive edema, necrosis, hemorrhagic blisters, severe local pain |

## Severity Classification
- **Dry Bite**: Fang punctures without local swelling, systemic envenomation, or bleeding (normal 20-min WBCT).
- **Mild**: Swelling limited to bite site (<1 joint crossed); no systemic envenomation.
- **Severe**: Neurotoxicity, systemic bleeding, hypotension, shock, or local swelling spreading rapidly past a major joint.

## Harmful Traditional Practices (DO NOT DO)
- **DO NOT** apply a tourniquet or tight ligature (triggers ischemic necrosis and gangrene).
- **DO NOT** incise, cut, cauterize, or attempt to suck out venom with mouth or pumps.
- **DO NOT** apply mud, cow dung, lime (chuna), snakestones, or herbal pastes to the wound.
- **DO NOT** immerse the bitten limb in ice water.

## First Aid & Call 112 Protocol
1. **Reassure the Victim**: Anxiety elevates heart rate, accelerating systemic venom absorption.
2. **Immobilize the Limb**: Splint limb in neutral position using a rigid board or rolled cloth, like a fracture. Keep bite site at or slightly below heart level.
3. **Remove Constrictive Items**: Remove rings, bangles, anklets, and tight garments immediately before edema sets in.
4. **Call 112 & Hospital Transport**: Transport directly to the nearest hospital or PHC stocked with polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom (ASV); your doctor may consider administration of polyvalent ASV based on clotting time and neurotoxic signs.

## ⚠️ Emergency Signs — Call 112
- Ptosis (inability to keep eyes open), blurred vision, or difficulty swallowing saliva
- Inability to count to 20 in a single breath or paradoxical breathing
- Continuous oozing of unclotted blood from fang marks or remote mucosal bleeding
- Rapidly progressive swelling advancing towards the groin or axilla
