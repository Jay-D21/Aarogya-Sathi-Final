# Acute Stroke Recognition and Emergency Protocol

## Overview & The FAST Acronym
Stroke occurs when blood supply to a part of the brain is interrupted, causing rapid neuronal necrosis. Every minute, 1.9 million neurons are lost.
The FAST screening protocol enables rapid public recognition:
- **F - Face Drooping**: One side of the face droops or is numb; uneven smile when smiling.
- **A - Arm Weakness**: One arm is weak or drifts downward when both arms are raised.
- **S - Speech Difficulty**: Slurred speech, inability to speak, or difficulty repeating a simple sentence.
- **T - Time to Call 112**: Note the exact symptom onset time and call 112 immediately.

## Diagnostic Criteria & Stroke Types
| Parameter | Ischemic Stroke (~80-85%) | Intracerebral Hemorrhage (~15-20%) |
|---|---|---|
| Etiology | Thrombus or embolus occluding cerebral artery | Ruptured cerebral vessel into brain tissue |
| Clinical Onset | Sudden focal neurological deficit | Sudden deficit accompanied by severe headache |
| Consciousness | Typically preserved initially | Early deterioration of consciousness, vomiting |
| Emergency Imaging | Immediate non-contrast brain CT / MRI | CT confirms hyperdense hematoma |

## Severity Classification
- **Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)**: Neurological symptoms resolving within 1-24 hours; extreme short-term stroke risk.
- **Minor Stroke**: Mild weakness, numbness, or dysarthria (NIHSS score ≤4).
- **Moderate to Severe Stroke**: Dense hemiplegia, aphasia, altered sensorium, or coma (NIHSS >15).

## India-Specific Context
- Stroke incidence is rising among younger adults in India due to high rates of uncontrolled hypertension, diabetes, and smokeless tobacco (gutkha/khaini).
- Delay risk: Families frequently wait overnight, mistakenly attributing arm or leg weakness to "sleeping in a bad posture" or "gas/nerve weakness".
- Post-acute dietary support: Emphasize low-salt, easy-to-swallow foods like moong dal khichdi, curd, soft roti, and vegetable sabzi; avoid sodium-heavy pickles and papad.

## Immediate Action & Call 112 Protocol
1. **Call 112 Immediately**: Request transport to a designated stroke-ready hospital equipped with 24/7 CT imaging.
2. **The Golden Hour**: Intravenous thrombolysis is highly time-sensitive (within 3 to 4.5 hours of onset); your doctor may consider thrombolytic therapy following CT confirmation.
3. **DO NOT Give Food, Water, or Oral Medications**: Swallowing reflex may be impaired, risking aspiration pneumonia.
4. **DO NOT Give Aspirin**: Aspirin given prior to a CT scan can prove fatal if the stroke is hemorrhagic.
5. **Positioning**: Keep the patient lying flat with head elevated 15-30 degrees; if vomiting, turn them into the lateral recovery position.

## ⚠️ Emergency Signs — Call 112
- Sudden numbness or paralysis of the face, arm, or leg (especially unilateral)
- Sudden confusion, difficulty speaking, or loss of comprehension
- Sudden loss of balance, vertigo, or severe unsteadiness
- Sudden explosive "thunderclap" headache with nausea
