# Hydration & Fluid Balance

## Baseline Requirements
ICMR 2023 guidelines recommend 2.5 to 3.5 liters of safe fluid intake daily for adults under average Indian climatic conditions. Fluid needs must be individualized: for conditions like heart failure or chronic kidney disease, your doctor may consider strict fluid and sodium restrictions.

## Indian Summer Heat & Electrolyte Depletion
During intense heatwaves (May–June) when daytime temperatures surpass 42°C–46°C, profuse sweating strips water and sodium, triggering heat exhaustion, hemoconcentration, and prerenal acute kidney injury.

## Rehydration Solutions & Electrolytes
| Rehydration Choice | Composition & Properties | Clinical Utility |
|---|---|---|
| **WHO-Formula ORS** | Exact sodium-glucose balance (75 mmol/L Na, 75 mmol/L glucose) | Gold standard for clinical dehydration, heat exhaustion, and acute diarrheal loss |
| **Traditional Chaas** | Diluted curd/buttermilk with roasted cumin (*jeera*) and curry leaves | Cooling probiotic electrolyte drink; replenishes potassium and minerals |
| **Nimbu Pani & Coconut Water** | Lemon water with a pinch of salt; tender coconut water | Natural potassium-rich hydration; avoid adding refined sugar for diabetics |

## Water Quality & Kidney Health
- **Nephrolithiasis (Kidney Stones)**: Chronic low-volume concentrated urine in hot climates sharply increases calcium oxalate crystal formation. Aim for pale straw-colored urine throughout the day.
- **Waterborne Pathogen Protection**: Untreated tap or borehole water poses high risks of amoebiasis, typhoid, and viral hepatitis. Ensure water is boiled or filtered (RO/UV) before consumption.

## Severe Heat Stroke & Dehydration Protocol (Call 112)
- **Emergency Signs**: High core body temperature (>40°C), cessation of sweating (hot, dry skin), confusion, seizures, or no urine output for >8 hours.
- Move patient immediately into shade, wet the skin with cool water, fan aggressively, and **Call 112** for emergency transport.
