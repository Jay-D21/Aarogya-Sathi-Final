# Dengue — ICMR Management Guidelines

## Definition
An acute mosquito-borne viral infection caused by the dengue virus (DENV-1 to 4) transmitted by *Aedes aegypti* mosquitoes, characterized by sudden high fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain, and myalgia.

## Diagnostic Criteria
| Test Phase | Diagnostic Marker | Clinical Timing |
|------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Day 1–5 (Viremic Phase) | NS1 Antigen (ELISA / Rapid) | Early detection during acute fever |
| Day 5 onwards | Dengue IgM ELISA | Positive during recovery / immune phase |
| Daily Monitoring | Hematocrit (HCT) & Platelet Count | Monitor hemoconcentration and thrombocytopenia |

## ICMR Severity Classification
- **Dengue Fever without Warning Signs**: Acute febrile illness with body aches, nausea, rash; normal vitals.
- **Dengue with Warning Signs**: Abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleeding, fluid accumulation.
- **Severe Dengue (DHF / DSS)**: Severe plasma leakage leading to shock or fluid overload, severe bleeding, or organ impairment (AST/ALT ≥1000).

## India-Specific Context & Clinical Management
- **Monsoon & Stagnant Water**: Vector breeding surges during and post-monsoon in coolers, discarded plastic containers, flower vases, and overhead tanks.
- **Hydration Protocol**: Aggressive oral fluids—WHO ORS, tender coconut water (*nariyal pani*), kanji, light moong dal soup. Avoid dark-colored liquids that obscure vomiting blood.
- **Medical Therapy**: Paracetamol for fever. **Strictly avoid** NSAIDs (ibuprofen, aspirin, diclofenac) due to hemorrhage risk. Your doctor may consider hospital admission for IV crystalloids.

## ⚠️ Hospitalization & Emergency Signs — Call 112 Immediately
- Persistent severe abdominal pain or repeated vomiting (>3 episodes in 24 hours)
- Spontaneous mucosal bleeding: epistaxis (nose bleed), gum bleed, black tarry stools (melena)
- Platelet count falling rapidly below 20,000/μL or rising hematocrit (>20% jump)
- Cold, clammy extremities, rapid feeble pulse, or sudden hypotension (Dengue Shock)
