# AQI Health Impact

## AQI Levels and Health Advice

| AQI Range | Category | Advice for NCD Patients |
|-----------|----------|------------------------|
| 0-50 | Good | Safe for all outdoor activities |
| 51-100 | Moderate | Sensitive groups limit prolonged outdoor exertion |
| 101-150 | Unhealthy (Sensitive) | Reduce outdoor exercise; asthmatics use inhalers |
| 151-200 | Unhealthy | Avoid outdoor exercise; exercise indoors |
| 201-300 | Very Unhealthy | Stay indoors; close windows |
| 300+ | Hazardous | Emergency: stay indoors, use air purifier if available |

## Impact on Specific Conditions

- **Diabetes**: High AQI (>150) increases insulin resistance. See [Type 2 Diabetes](../conditions/diabetes-type2.md)
- **Hypertension**: PM2.5 exposure raises blood pressure acutely. See [Hypertension](../conditions/hypertension.md)
- **COPD/Asthma**: Even moderate AQI can trigger exacerbations
- **Heart Disease**: PM2.5 increases cardiovascular event risk

## India-Specific Context

- Delhi NCR: AQI regularly exceeds 300 in winter (Nov-Feb)
- Mumbai: Coastal humidity + traffic = persistent PM2.5 above 100
- Shirpur/Rural Maharashtra: Generally better AQI but seasonal crop burning affects air quality

## Recommendations for High AQI Days

1. Exercise indoors (yoga, stretching, bodyweight exercises)
2. Keep windows closed during peak pollution hours (7-10 AM, 6-9 PM)
3. Use N95 masks outdoors if AQI > 200
4. Increase water intake to help clear airways
5. Asthma patients should keep rescue inhaler accessible
