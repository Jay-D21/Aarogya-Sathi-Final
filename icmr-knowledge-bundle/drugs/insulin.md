# Insulin Therapy

## Overview & Role
Exogenous peptide hormone essential for cellular glucose uptake. Used when lifestyle measures and oral antidiabetic agents fail to achieve individualized glycemic targets, or during metabolic decompensation in [Type 2 Diabetes](../conditions/diabetes-type2.md).

## Clinical Considerations
Your doctor may consider basal, bolus, or premixed insulin regimens tailored to your glycemic profile, HbA1c levels, and dietary carbohydrate distribution.

## Types of Insulin
| Type | Onset | Peak | Clinical Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rapid / Short-Acting | 15-30 min | 1-3 hrs | Mealtime coverage; taken before dal-roti or rice |
| Intermediate / Long-Acting | 1-2 hrs | Flat / Minimal | Provides 24-hour basal background insulin coverage |
| Premixed (Biphasic) | Dual | Dual | Common in India; provides basal and mealtime coverage twice daily |

## Subcutaneous Injection Sites
- **Primary Sites**: Abdomen (fastest, most predictable absorption; keep 2 inches from navel), anterior/lateral thighs, upper outer arms, buttocks.
- **Site Rotation**: Rotate injection points within the same anatomical zone weekly to prevent lipohypertrophy (fatty lumps that impair absorption).

## Safe Storage in Indian Heat
- **Unopened Vials/Pens**: Keep refrigerated between 2°C and 8°C (never freeze).
- **In-Use Vials/Pens**: May be kept at room temperature (<30°C) away from direct sunlight for up to 28-30 days.
- **Extreme Indian Summer (>40°C) / Rural Households**: If refrigeration is unavailable during heatwaves or power cuts, store insulin in an unglazed earthenware clay pot (*matka*) surrounded by damp sand and water. Never leave insulin inside a car, metal box, or near a kitchen stove (*chulha*).

## Hypoglycemia Risk & Management
- Skipping or delaying meals (such as morning paratha or evening khichdi) after insulin injection carries significant hypoglycemia risk.
- **Mild Hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL)**: Take 15g fast-acting sugar (3 tsp sugar in water or half cup fruit juice); retest in 15 minutes.
- **Emergency Signs (Call 112)**: Unconsciousness, confusion, or inability to swallow. Do NOT attempt to pour liquids into mouth; call 112 immediately.
